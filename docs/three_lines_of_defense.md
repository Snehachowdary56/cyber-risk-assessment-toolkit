# The 3 lines of defense model, and where this toolkit fits

The **3 lines of defense** model is a common way to split risk management duties so that no one checks their own work.

| Line | Who | What they do for cyber risk | In this toolkit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st line of defense** | The business and IT teams that own and run a system (the **assessment requestors**) | Own the risk day to day, answer the questionnaire, fix gaps, keep their **risk register** up to date | They submit requests and receive a **risk briefing** with actions |
| **2nd line of defense** | Digital risk and compliance (the **facilitator**) | Set the method, review requests, assign the assessment level, run the assessment, challenge answers, report to senior leaders | Intake tracking, level assignment, scoring, reports, dashboard |
| **3rd line of defense** | Internal audit | Independently checks that the 1st and 2nd lines work as designed | Reads the risk register and intake log as evidence |

## What a 2nd line facilitator does with this toolkit

1. **Record** each cyber risk assessment request in the intake tracking system. Incomplete or duplicate requests go back to the requestor with the reason.
2. **Review** the request and assign the **assessment level** (Basic, Intermediate or Advanced). The reasons are written out so the 1st line can see why.
3. **Run the questionnaire** for that level. Higher levels ask more questions.
4. **Score** each gap: likelihood x impact gives Low, Medium, High or Critical.
5. **Brief** the requestor on the top risks and the actions needed (risk briefing).
6. **Document** the full risk assessment report and add the risks to the risk register.
7. **Report** to senior leaders through the dashboard: turnaround times, overdue requests, risks by rating and compliance by NIST CSF function.

The facilitator advises and challenges; the 1st line still owns the risk and decides, with sign-off from a leader, whether to fix or accept it.
